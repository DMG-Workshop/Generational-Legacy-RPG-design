# Roadmap

## Text game: the queue

In order:

1. **Feats.** Done. Non-spell damage feats, feat points and slots, loadouts changed only in towns or after a rest. Includes the **auto-battle toggle**: once turned on, Hunt and Hard hunt fight themselves until the player turns it off.
2. **Battle-side diseases.** Done. Looting corpses, coating blades with toxins, throwing plague vials, sickness spreading between companions.
3. **Whole-game bug hunt.** Done. UI, saves, balance and long runs, across every system.
4. **Skill trees.** Done. Trees for the heir and for companions, with some nodes passing down the bloodline.
5. **Mobile: Android and iOS.** Done, apart from testing on a real device. The same Godot project, exported to phones and tablets. The game logic and data stay as they are; the work is in the screens:
   - Export setup for Android (built on Linux with the Android SDK and a signing key) and iOS (built on a Mac with Xcode; installing on an iPhone or publishing needs an Apple Developer account).
   - Touch instead of hover: everything that shows details on mouse hover (traits, feat and spell previews, tavern, shop and map details) also shows them on tap-and-hold.
   - Readable on a phone: a text-size setting and larger buttons, with layouts checked on small screens.
   - Landscape lock, so the 1280x720 layout fills the screen.
   - Saves, auto-battle and the autopilot checked on a real device.
6. **Party commands.** Done. In manual mode a battle with companions steps through each member (the heir, then every companion, from their own class skills, spells, feats and the shared potions), queues the choices and plays the round together on Fight!. Per-member Auto (remembered), Repeat last round, Back, a Flee that covers the party, and a party strip showing who is asked and what is queued. A solo hunt and the master Auto-battle play exactly as before.

Also done: the autopilot takes Hard hunts once its heir is clearly strong, which keeps battle deaths in the 3-7% band now that skill trees make heirs stronger.

23. **Save versioning.** Done (`GameSaveVersion`). A save version number and a chain of tested upgrade steps from each version to the next. Today old saves load because new keys are optional; world chunks, inventories and positions need more than that.
24. **Saving in the middle of a fight.** Done (`GameDynasty.to_save`, `GameBattle` and `GameRound` `to_dict`/`restore`). A battle, and a round waiting on a question, saved and loaded exactly. A phone can close the app at any moment, and the sandbox needs mid-action state anyway.
25. **A fixed list of events for views.** Done for the battle: every typed event a fight emits is listed with its fields and kinds in `data/game/battle_events.json` (`GameEventSpec`), and `tests/test_game_event_list.gd` checks that each event played and each type in the source is on the list. Life actions return text lines and emit no typed events; they move to keyed data with item 28. The isometric view becomes a second renderer on the same events as the text screens.
26. **Cosmetic randomness kept apart.** Done: the view's dice are `CosmeticRng`'s (`ui/play/cosmetic_rng.gd`), and `tests/test_game_cosmetic.gd` checks that nothing in `ui/play` draws the dynasty's or Godot's global dice and that fights shown on the battle screen end exactly as fights with no view. Today only the scenery scatter rolls dice (keyed by place); particles and camera shake will use the same class.
27. **A world clock apart from actions.** Done (`GameClock`, `GameDynasty.pass_time`): one call passes time, whether an action passes whole years or a real-time world feeds seconds, which add up to year-long spans with the rest carried and saved. A span of 1 year and 60 seconds a year are placeholders (`balance.json` "clock_*") to tune with the sandbox; shorter spans would need the per-call rolls (weather, the death roll, mana regeneration) scaled to the span first.

Still to move: the life actions' messages (`game_dynasty.gd`, `game_events.gd`, `game_party.gd`, `game_house.gd` and the rest of `core/game`, about 140 more sites). The battle events list (item 25) is part of the data check.
29. **Replay files.** Done (`GameReplay`, `tools/replay.gd`): a run's founding and commands, with fingerprints, as one pasteable line, played back to exactly the same dynasty or to the command where it first differs. The autopilot and the tests record; the screens do not yet, so a player's own session cannot be captured until each screen's actions go through the recorder (the next step for player bug reports). The balance harness can use it to keep a seed's run as a regression.

It can be started by hand with other seeds and generations. Replaying a failing seed uses item 29.

Export to file and Import from file on the title screen (and Export to file in the game menu) write and read a `.glsave` text file holding the same block as Copy backup (`GameSaves.export_file`/`import_file`), through the platform's file dialog where it has one and otherwise through the Downloads or `user://exports` folder. **Before the 2D/3D sandbox: world systems, settled in text first**

The text game is the cheap place to get these rules right: they are core rules and data with headless tests, and a text screen where one helps.

41. **World spaces and coordinates.** The roadmap's world-space IDs given to towns, dungeons, the house and interiors now, with map positions, so travel already speaks the sandbox's language.
42. **One item and material list.** Done (`GameMaterials`, `data/items/materials.json`): 21 materials (tier 1 to 4, with tags such as metal, wood, cloth, precious, light, heavy, magic), and every item in `items.json` now names a `material` and a `weight`; its tags are the material's plus its slot or kind (`GameItems.tags_of`, `has_tag`, `material_of`, `weight_of`). The validator (in `tools/check_data.gd`) wants every item to reach a listed material, a weight, a tier in range, gear that does not stack and consumables that do, and every material and tag used. Nothing the game plays changed. Stable IDs are the existing lower-case ids, checked. Harvesting, mining, crafting (items 43 to 45) and the loot generator build on this list.
43. **Inventory with stacks and weight.** First part built (`GameCarry`, `data/items/carry.json`, on the weights of item 42): the heir carries what is worn, what is in the pack and a little for each healing potion, and can carry up to 40 plus 0.6 times their Strength. Past that nothing more is picked up by choice: a purchase is refused (the reason shown), and a find (the search of the dead, a made drop) is sold where it lies; gifts from events and quests are always taken, and may leave the heir over the limit with no other penalty. Stacks were already there (remedies, toxins and vials stack to their `stack` size) and each unit weighs its own. The pack screen has a Drop button (gear asks twice) and says how much is carried; the life screen's summary too. The autopilot is never over the limit (a test plays it), so its results and the golden digests do not move. Still to do: a penalty or a speed cost for a heavy load, carrying for the party and the house's stores, containers and bags that raise the limit, scrapping into materials (item 44), and weight of the creatures and goods the sandbox will move.
44. **Gathering and crafting.** First part built (`GameCraft`, `data/items/craft.json`, `ui/play/craft_panel.gd`). **There is no recipe grid: a piece is composed.** The heir picks a form, a material they have gathered and up to three essences, and the result is the same kind of made item the loot generator builds (an id `GameLoot.build` reads), so what is made and what drops are one system. Essences are affixes kept in a bottle: 0, 1, 2 or 3 of them make a common, fine, rare or legendary piece (a mythic cannot be crafted). **Gathering:** hunts leave materials (a table per creature, rolled on dice keyed on the seed, asked for by the screens only), a slain legend always leaves its parts, and now and then a creature leaves an essence (the wolf leaves the Wolf). **Salvaging:** break up gear in the pack for half its material and, from a made piece, its affixes as essences (a pelt keeps the wolf), so a full pack becomes parts. The stores belong to the family and pass down (`GameDynasty.stores`, saved only once something is in them). Crafting needs a forge (a town); salvaging works anywhere. Cost: material by the piece's weight, plus a fee of 15% of its price in gold. Quality 0 to 9 comes from the heir's level, Craft quality traits (Craftmaster's Hands, Artificer) and a keyed roll, and is shown before the piece is made; a made piece takes the world's grade. The workshop opens from the House panel. The autopilot never gathers or makes anything (tested). Still to do: foraging and harvesting as actions (wood, plants, ore: the sandbox's gathering), a workshop house track and Craft class perks (speed, the Enchanter, Alchemist and Blacksmith lines), repairing and upgrading a piece, making consumables, and crafting in the world rather than a menu.
45. **Mining as data.** First part built, headless (`GameMine`, `data/mining/mining.json`; no screen yet). **The ground:** rock layers by depth (topsoil, clay, stone, deep stone, darkrock, ember rock, worldroot) with a hardness and whether ore forms there, grouped into the GDD's three zones (the Surface, Underground, the Deep), down to depth 400; and digging down goes back in time (`age_at`: the dynasty's own Age at the surface, one older for each 60 deep, none before the first Age). **Ore** grows in veins: each 8 by 8 by 8 block may hold one vein, a sphere whose kind comes from depth bands (copper and iron near the surface, then crystal and silver, gold, mithril, and starmetal only in the Deep), so the deeper it is the rarer and better: about 7.6% of cells near the surface are ore, 0.65% at the bottom. All of it is a pure function of the dynasty seed and a **site** (a place, by id): the same seed, the same ground; no dice of the dynasty's are drawn. **Digging** a cell takes effort (the layer's hardness against the digger's Strength), needs open air beside it (the surface at depth 0, or an earlier cell, so a shaft is a connected hole), and puts any ore into the family's stores (item 44). **Digging changes the ground:** each stroke adds strain to the shaft's 16 by 16 region, more the deeper it is; through the stages (Quiet, Tremors, Cracked, Weeping, Hollow, Open) the rock cuts easier and the veins swell, and the last stage opens a **gateway** to the underworld at the cell that tipped it. The dug cells are saved as runs per column, only once something is dug. The autopilot never digs (tested). Still to do: a text screen for a shaft, the underworld as a space of its own behind the gateway (item 41), the Ancient Vaults and buried ruins of earlier Ages, tools and tiers, mining perks (Stonekin sense ore, the Miner job), hazards (collapse, gas, flooding), and world generation of the surface (item 46).
46. **World generation from the dynasty's seed.** Biomes, terrain, strata and the placement of nodes and ores, the same for the same seed, checked headless.
47. **Spawn tables.** Which creatures appear where and when, by biome, depth and Age, as data; hunts can use them before the world does.
48. **Creature behaviour as data.** Wander, chase, flee, guard and swarm rules by tag, tested headless, so creatures in the open world need no hand-written code each.
49. **Pathfinding over dug and built ground.** A grid and height pathfinder that digging and building change, with headless tests.
50. **Building and placement rules.** What can stand where (support, height, rooms), grown from the family house (item 21), which becomes the first base.
51. **Day, night and weather.** A cycle on the world clock (item 27), with weather that changes hunts and the look of the world; battles already have weather.
52. **Companions in the world.** Following, idling and helping (gathering, guarding), built on the party rules.
53. **Sandbox tips.** The first-heir tips extended to moving, digging and building, from data like today's.
54. **Desktop builds and crash reports.** Windows, Linux and macOS exports with gamepad menus, store images from the screenshot tool, and opt-in error reports that carry a save, so problems on phones can be reproduced.

**Creatures and titles (added to the queue)**

Finding and raising creatures (eggs and young), ranching, breeding, and titles that can be gained. Design notes, and what each word means here, are in `docs/GDD.md` ("Eggs, Young, Ranching and Breeding" and "Titles"). None of these is built. Each draws its dice from its own seeded stream (as `GameSex` does), never the dynasty's main dice, and the autopilot never searches for young, raises, breeds or picks a title, so the golden digests and the balance figures do not move until a later step makes creatures fight for the autopilot, which would be a balance change with its own numbers.

55. **Finding eggs and young, and a creature roster.** Creatures are not tamed; they are found young and raised. Each creature in `data/creatures` says how it is born: it **lays eggs** (dragons, drakes, birds, reptiles, spiders) or bears **live young** (wolves, bears, cats), and what its young are called (egg, then hatchling or whelp; pup, cub, kit, foal). Young are found in the places creatures live: a nest or lair after a hunt or an Explore event, a clutch beside a slain mother, a litter left behind. The chance comes from the place and the creature (a legend's clutch is rare and guarded), and is better with the Beast Tamer job, the right traits and a tracking-minded class. A found egg must be kept warm until it hatches (years on the world clock; neglect spoils it); found young must be fed and sheltered. A creature raised from the egg or the cub is bonded to the heir who raised it, with no roll to tame it, and its bond grows with care. Raised creatures join a roster saved on the dynasty (only once something is found, so old saves load as before), with a name, sex, age, traits and a temperament, and can go out with the party as beasts beside the companions. Builds on the party rules and the Hall (item 21). Rules and data first, with a text screen and headless tests. Done so far: each creature's way of being born and the name of its young are in `data/creatures/creatures.json` (8 lay eggs, 7 bear live young, 14 are not born and are never found young), read through `GameBirth`. **Found beside the dead**: after the dead are searched, each foe that was born may have left an egg or young (`GameFinding`, `data/creatures/finding.json`), on its own seeded dice, better for a ranger, druid or beastmaster; the find goes to a saved roster (`GameRoster`, six places for now) and is told in the journal. The Chronicle has a **Creatures tab** listing what is kept. **Growth and keep** (item 56, first part) are built: creatures age through egg, young, adult and elder as the world clock passes and die of old age (`GameRanch`, `data/creatures/ranch.json`), cost gold a year from the heir, and are neglected if it runs out (an egg spoils, a creature wanders off). **Also built:** Explore events that give young (a nest on a ledge, a litter in a den; offered only to a house that has found young before), a chance of the young of a slain legend that is born (Grimfang, Vaerthax), and a find bonus for Wild Instincts and the faetouched. Still to do: the Beast Tamer job's effect (it needs the subclass mechanic, item 60), and young going out with the party.
56. **The ranch.** *(Done so far: growth, keep and neglect; the Ranch house track with pens, a hatchery and a nursery; selling and setting free. Work is built too: wool, eggs and venom pay gold, guards cut what events take. Raids (61) and farm animals (62) come later.)* A third track for the family house (after the Hall and the Yard): pens that hold creatures, feeding and care paid in gold and food each year through the world clock (`pass_time`, item 27), a hatchery where eggs are kept warm until they hatch, a nursery for young, a mood that rises and falls with care, and a life of its own (egg or newborn, young, adult, elder, death of old age). The ranch passes down to the next heir like the rest of the house. Creatures there can be sent out as party beasts, put to work (wool, eggs, venom, guarding), sold or set free.
57. **Breeding: hybrids and cross-breeds.** Two creatures in a pen can have young, rolled from the ranch's own seeded dice. The same species breed true, passing on traits, with a chance of a mutation; a **hybrid** is a pairing the game defines (a recipe in data, with a species, stats and art of its own, such as a wolf and a drake making an ember hound); a **cross-breed** is any other pairing of kin species that can breed at all, an individual carrying both lines, with traits from each, lower fertility, and sometimes sterile. Which species can breed is data (a family and tags per creature), so new creatures need no new code. Named lines are kept across generations, as the dynasty keeps its own. Art for cross-breeds is an open question (the dominant parent's sprite with the other's colours is the cheap answer). Done so far: **how a mixed pairing is born is decided** (`GameBirth`, `data/creatures/breeding.json`): a hybrid's recipe says for itself, a cross-breed follows its mother, and the young are named for the parent whose way they follow; the rule is one word of data (`mother`, `father`, `egg` or `live`) and the hybrid recipes are a list in that file, empty until the ranch brings its hybrids. **Built so far (first part):** the player sets a grown creature to breed and it pairs each year with a compatible, able creature of the opposite sex (`GameBreeding`, `data/creatures/breeding_rules.json`): the same species breeds true, kin of a family make a **cross-breed** (it carries both lines, is less fertile and often sterile), and a pair with a recipe makes a **hybrid** (the engine is there; no recipes ship yet, they need new creature data and art). Young keep the mother's species and line, inherit traits (hardy, sickly, fierce, prized, industrious, fruitful) and can mutate one; the mother rests between litters. Still to do: choosing pairs, the shipped hybrids with art, and naming lines.
58. **Cross-world creatures.** Finding, raising and breeding across the realms: each realm has creatures of its own (fae beasts in the Verdant Court, hounds of the Hollow Below, drakes of the Dragon Isles, elementals, dream and void creatures, constructs of the Clockwork Expanse), a creature can be taken to another realm and may need to adapt to it, and creatures from different realms can be crossed, the young carrying traits of both worlds (some stable, some unstable, as the Void's are). Creatures get a `realm` now, so the text game can already reward them; crossing realms needs the gates and teleport circles of the world spaces (item 41 and the sandbox's "Worlds and spaces"), so the rules are written in text first and switched on when the worlds are linked.
59. **Titles that can be gained.** First part done (`GameTitles`, `data/titles/titles.json`, the Chronicle's Titles tab): 27 titles, 18 personal and 9 house, earned from what the game already counts (battles won, a clean streak, level, gold, children, companions, spells, the legends slain by this heir or by the house, the generation, the Age, the years, the heirlooms and the Hall and Yard). Each is read from the dynasty's own state, so old saves earn what they deserve; only the ids earned are saved. The heir wears one at a time after the name ("Tess Holloway, the Untouched"); a personal title ends with its heir and stays in their lineage record, a house title stays with the house; some give a small perk (cheaper shops, only while worn). The screens ask for titles (the autopilot never does, so no result moves). Still to come as their systems arrive: the ranch's titles ("Master Breeder", "Warden of a Hundred Years", items 55 to 58), noble titles and land claims through faction reputation (item 17), job ranks, and awarding titles from achievements (item 16); more perks (the chance of finding young, item 55) need those systems too. A new condition is a branch in `GameTitles.met` and `_cond_problems`.
60. **The Breeder: a subclass anyone can take.** Raising creatures is a subclass layered on the heir's class, so any heir can take it; an heir who **starts as a Breeder** is better at it, and there is a chance of **advanced subclasses of it** (egg-keeper, pack-master, crossbreeder, warden of old lines) or of a child **born to a full class of it**. First the general subclass mechanic, which the game does not have yet: a subclass is data (`data/subclasses`), the heir holds none or one, it is saved only when taken, it adds small bonuses to what the class already does and never replaces the class's skills, and it can be taken or changed at stated costs. Then the Breeder itself, whose bonuses read from the roster and ranch (items 55 to 57): finding young, hatching and raising, mutation and hybrid odds, upkeep. The advanced subclasses open from ranch size, lines kept and titles (item 59), and the full class is born by the same chance rule as the hybrid classes. The Beast Tamer and Familiar Breeder jobs fold into it; the existing Beastmaster class stays and is the model for the full class. Builds on 55 to 57 and 59. Balance-affecting once beasts fight, so it is measured with the harness then. **First part done:** the subclass mechanic (`GameSubclass`, `data/subclasses`: none or one per heir, picked at the founding, taken later for gold, or passed to a child at 50%; founded perks are 1.5 times stronger) and the **Breeder** with five perks (finding young 40% more, keep 15% cheaper, breeding 25% more fertile, mutations 50% likelier, creatures sell 15% dearer). Still to do: the advanced subclasses of the Breeder and the full class born to it, the Beast Tamer and Familiar Breeder jobs folding into it, more subclasses for other trades, and recording the founding subclass in replays.
61. **Raids and theft on the house.** Threats to what the family owns: thieves after the purse, raiders after the herd, beasts after the pens, rivals who bear a grudge. They come on the world clock (`pass_time`) with their own seeded dice, never the dynasty's, and how likely they are depends on what there is to take (gold held, animals kept, the ranch's size) and on the family's name (renown, infamy, faction standing). Guards stand in the way: guard creatures (item 56's guards already turn away a share of the gold an event would take), hired guards and later walls and gates. A raid ends one of three ways: the guards beat it off, it takes something (gold, an animal, a pen's worth of damage), or the heir rides out and fights it as a battle. Raids are only possible once there is something worth raiding (a ranch or a stock of animals), so a house that keeps none is never raided and the autopilot's results do not move; if raids ever reach a plain life that would be a balance change and would need numbers. Pairs with items 56 and 62; guards that fight raise the guard creature's value to the house. **Built:** a house with animals in the pens or a house that has been built (the Hall, the Yard, the Ranch) can be raided each year (more with more to take, less with guards), by thieves (gold), burglars (a carried item), rustlers (an animal) or beasts (kill one), as an event with four ways out: trust the guards (they may beat it off), ride out and fight, pay, or a trick (a skill check, falling back on the guards). A plain house is raided too, at a small flat chance, once the heir holds 100 gold or more (a balance change: about 0.8 raids a life for the autopilot; battle deaths 3.8% before, 4.1% after). Still to do: renown and infamy changing the chance, hired guards, walls and gates, more raiders and rival houses, and raids on a plain house.
62. **Farm animals and their worth.** The ranch keeps farm animals as well as wild creatures: sheep, goats, cattle, pigs, chickens, geese, horses and bees. They are not fought and not found beside the dead; they are bought young at the market, bred (item 57) and raised like any creature (item 56's stages, keep and mood). Each has a worth that comes from what it gives: **wool** and **milk** and **eggs** every year, **meat** and **hide** once, when it is slaughtered, and live sale by stage and condition as now. The yields are data per species (`gold` or a good with a market price), vary a little with the season and the market the heir sells at, and a poorly kept animal gives less. Slaughter is a deliberate choice (asks twice, like selling), gives meat and hide and ends the animal. Farm animals are what raiders want (item 61) and what guard creatures protect. Needs creature data with a `farm` flag (kept out of fight and Explore pools), a price list of goods, and art for each animal. Item 56's wool, eggs and venom work is a stand-in: wool is on the frost wolf only because the game has no sheep yet, and moves to the sheep when they exist. **Built:** seven farm animals (sheep, cattle, pigs, chickens, goats, geese, horses, with art; a horse hauls and is never slaughtered), kept out of every fight and find; bought young at a town's market; raised and bred like any creature; wool, milk and eggs by the season and by how well they are kept; slaughter for meat and hide (asks twice). Wool moved from the frost wolf to the sheep. Still to do: bees (a hive needs its own rules), prices that vary with the market, trade, and the goods having a market of their own.
63. **A loot generator with no item list.** First part built (`GameLoot`, `data/items/loot.json`, on the material list of item 42). A made item is built from its own id, `gen:<rarity>:<slot>:<form>:<material>:<affixes>:<mythic>:<grade>:<quality>`, out of 15 forms, the materials, 24 affixes (prefix and suffix) and 6 rarities, so a save holds only the id and `GameItems.item_def` hands the built item to everything that knows items (wearing, selling, tags, weapon kind, saving). **It grows with the world:** material tiers open at generations 1, 6, 14 and 30, and after that every 12 generations add a grade (+1, +2, up to +20) that strengthens all effects, so there is always something better. **Rarity** is a roll out of a million: common 70%, fine 23%, rare 6%, epic 0.9%, legendary 0.099%, and **mythic ten in a million**, with a name of its own (Dawnbringer, Aegis of Ages, the First Crown) and the strongest effects in the game; a slain legend leaves one drop at 25 times the odds of the rare ones. Drops are asked for by the screens only (the search of the dead, a slain legend), on dice keyed on the seed, so the autopilot and the golden digests do not move. Still to do: art for made gear (it keeps the old block look), a way to scrap or sort a full pack, more forms and affixes, drops from events, chests and shops, crafting and enchanting on the same parts (item 44), and a choice of how generous drops should be after play-testing.

## After the text game: the 2D/3D sandbox

Starts only once the text game is complete. The text game's systems (lineage, Fate, battles, diseases, companions, Ages) carry over: logic stays apart from rendering, so the new world is a new view on the same core.

### Look and shape
- An **open-world sandbox**, in the spirit of Minecraft, Terraria and RimWorld.
- **Isometric pixel-art 2D, running on 3D code**: a real 3D world drawn as isometric pixel art, so height, digging down and building up are real.

### Harvesting
- Wood, rock, plants, food and fruit, meat from creatures, ores, minerals, gems and many kinds of fluids.
- **Finite nodes that come back.** A tree or an ore vein gives a few items, then disappears. It respawns after a while, unless the player has built over the spot.

### Mining
- Mining is a headline system that should go beyond any other mining game: depth, strata, what lies under the ground and how digging changes the world.
- **Digging into the underworld.** Mining down from the overworld slowly changes the ground around the shaft until it becomes a gateway: the player passes from the overworld into the underworld and the two connect there.

### Crafting
- A **one-of-a-kind crafting system**, designed to stand on its own rather than copy a recipe grid.

### Worlds and spaces
- **Gateways to other worlds** (the realms).
- **World-space IDs.** Every space has its own ID. Entering a building loads its interior as its own space, like a small dimension of its own. The same mechanism carries the underworld and the other worlds.
- **Creatures across the worlds.** Raised and bred creatures travel by the same gateways, and can be crossed with creatures of other realms (roadmap item 58): the ranch is a place in a world, and a pen's creatures live by that world's flow of time.

### Mods
- **Mod support.** Players can load their own mods or other people's, each in their own game. Content is already data-driven (JSON), which is the base for this.
