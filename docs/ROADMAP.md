# Roadmap

## Text game: the queue

In order:

1. **Feats.** Done. Non-spell damage feats, feat points and slots, loadouts changed only in towns or after a rest. Includes the **auto-battle toggle**: once turned on, Hunt and Hard hunt fight themselves until the player turns it off.
2. **Battle-side diseases.** Done. Looting corpses, coating blades with toxins, throwing plague vials, sickness spreading between companions.
3. **Whole-game bug hunt.** Done. UI, saves, balance and long runs, across every system.
4. **Skill trees.** Done. Trees for the heir and for companions, with some nodes passing down the bloodline.
5. **Mobile: Android and iOS.** Done, apart from testing on a real device. The same Godot project, exported to phones and tablets. The game logic and data stay as they are; the work is in the screens:
   - Export setup for Android (built on Linux with the Android SDK and a signing key) and iOS (built on a Mac with Xcode; installing on an iPhone or publishing needs an Apple Developer account).
   - Touch instead of hover: everything that shows details on mouse hover (traits, feat and spell previews, tavern, shop and map details) also shows them on tap-and-hold.
   - Readable on a phone: a text-size setting and larger buttons, with layouts checked on small screens.
   - Landscape lock, so the 1280x720 layout fills the screen.
   - Saves, auto-battle and the autopilot checked on a real device.
6. **Party commands.** Done. In manual mode a battle with companions steps through each member (the heir, then every companion, from their own class skills, spells, feats and the shared potions), queues the choices and plays the round together on Fight!. Per-member Auto (remembered), Repeat last round, Back, a Flee that covers the party, and a party strip showing who is asked and what is queued. A solo hunt and the master Auto-battle play exactly as before.

Also done: the autopilot takes Hard hunts once its heir is clearly strong, which keeps battle deaths in the 3-7% band now that skill trees make heirs stronger.

**Player experience**

Done: a one-line tip strip above the life screen's actions names the next thing to do and marks its button, and the succession screen carries the last tip. The tips come from the game's own state (so old saves need nothing), are written in `data/ui/tutorial.json`, and are switched off by Skip tips, by choosing the first heir, or under Menu > Tutorial tips.
11. **Several save slots and a backup export.** Done: three slots on the title screen (slot 1 is the existing save file), each saved atomically with a `.bak` it falls back to, and a text backup that Copy backup puts on the clipboard (title screen and game menu) and Restore backup reads back into any slot. Covering or erasing a house asks first. Backups pass through the clipboard only; a file export for phones is not built.
12. **A family tree viewer:** ancestors, traits, deaths and legacy echoes. Progress: a Family Tree tab in the chronicle shows each generation with parents, spouse, children, deaths, traits and the echoes from that generation (`GameFamilyTree`). Heirs folded into the Ages are counted, not named.
13. **A dynasty statistics screen:** deaths by cause, best heir, Ages reached, gold over time. Progress: a Statistics tab in the chronicle shows heirs lived, generations and Age, deaths by cause, legends slain, the greatest heir and the gold of each heir kept in full (`GameStats`). Gold over time: each Age keeps the total gold its heirs had at death, so the Statistics tab shows the average per Age, folded heirs included. An Age saved before this shows as not recorded.
14. **Accessibility options:** colour-blind palette, high contrast, reduced motion. Progress: the menu's Accessibility row turns on colour-blind colours (blue and orange for good and bad), high contrast (brighter quiet text) and reduced motion (no sliding or lunging in fights). The choices are stored in settings.cfg, not in the save.
15. **Sound and music:** battle cues, a death sting, ambient tracks per Age. Built, with every sound made by the game itself: there are no audio files, each cue and track is a few notes described in `data/ui/sound.json` and turned into sound the first time it is needed (`ui/play/sound.gd`). In a fight a blow, a critical blow, a hurt, a dodge, a heal, a spell, a guard, a foe falling, a companion knocked out and an escape each have a short cue, and a won or lost fight ends with one. An heir's death is shown with a sting and the saga's end with a different one. Quiet looping music plays on the title, in a fight, and for each Age of the world (four tracks, going round again in later Ages). The menu has Sound effects and Music rows, each Off, Low, Medium or High, kept in settings.cfg and not in the save; both start on Medium. Sound never changes a fight: the same fight ends the same heard or silent. Not built: recorded music or instrument samples, crossfades between tracks, sounds for buttons and for life actions (hunting, training, events), and **nobody has listened to it on a real device yet**: it was built and tested where there is no audio output, so the notes and volumes are a first guess to be tuned by ear.

**Game systems**
16. **Achievements and dynasty milestones.** Built (`GameAchievements`, `data/achievements/achievements.json`, the Chronicle's Milestones tab). 26 milestones that belong to the whole house: generations (5 to 1,000), years the house has stood (50 to 1,000), Ages seen, legends slain, heirs who have lived, the family house built up, creatures kept and bred, pieces made at the forge, pieces built, heirlooms, titles held, a million gold, and a dozen other milestones. A milestone is kept for good once reached, with the heir, generation and year, and is told in the journal; the tab shows how far along the rest are. Three bring a house title (item 59): the Millennial House (a thousand years), Bane of Legends (legends slain ten times over) and the Stockmen (three creatures bred at the ranch). Reaching is asked for by the screens, as titles are, so the autopilot reaches nothing and nothing in a life changes. Not built: rewards other than titles.
17. **Faction reputation in the text game**, so NPCs judge the heir by their ancestors. First part built (`GameFactions`, `data/factions/factions.json`, the Chronicle's Factions tab). Six factions, the Mage's Circle, the Thieves' Guild, the Warrior's Order, the Church, the Draconic Council and the Noble Houses, each hold the heir at a standing from -100 to 100, worked out from what the house remembers: what each kept ancestor was (their class), did (level, battles, kills, gold, titles) and died of, fading with every generation at the faction's own pace (the Church and the dragons forget slowly, the Guild fast), plus the living heir's own deeds and the house's glory, infamy, slain legends and milestones. A line of warriors is trusted by the Order; a line of necromancers is held warily by the Church and well by the Circle; the Council does not forgive Vaerthax. The tab shows each standing, its rank (hostile to honoured) and what the faction remembers. A faction that trusts the heir gives them a gift once in their life, in a town (gold, potions or XP, doubled when honoured), and at honoured confers a title: Magister of the Circle, Shadow of the Guild, Knight of the Order, the Blessed, Peer of the Realm. **Second part built: in the four towns that a faction keeps, the heir's standing with that faction moves shop and temple prices, companions' hire fees and the XP of training, by rank (a hostile town charges 15% more and teaches 15% slower; an honoured one charges 10% less, fees 20% less, and teaches 15% faster). Away from those towns nothing changes. **Not built, on purpose:** standings do not change quests or who attacks yet; each would change what the autopilot meets, so it needs the harness numbers before and after.
18. **Heirloom upgrades across generations.** Built (`GameHeirlooms`, `data/items/heirlooms.json`, the Heirlooms button on the family house). Each heirloom taken from a legend can be reworked at a forge, once in each heir's life: it goes from as it was taken to reforged, inlaid, rune-cut, storied and legendary, and every level adds 1% power for every heir after, on top of the 3% it always gave. The price is 400 gold for the first level and rises with the square of the level and with the generation. Because an heir can take each heirloom only one level further, it takes five generations to finish one, and the heirloom remembers the heirs who worked it. The autopilot reworks nothing, so nothing in its lives changes. Not built: heirlooms that do more than give power, and heirlooms from anything but a legend.
19. **Random world events per Age:** wars, famines and golden ages that change hunts, shops and diseases. Built (`GameWorldEvents`, `data/world/world_events.json`). Five kinds of event come and go with the years, one at a time: the War of the River Lords (prices up a quarter, fights pay 30% more gold), the Lean Years (prices up by half, work pays a fifth less, sickness takes hold more easily), the Grey Pestilence (sickness takes hold two and a half times as easily), the Great Fair (prices down a tenth, fights pay a little more) and, from the third generation of an Age, a Golden Age (prices down a fifth, fights teach more, work pays more). In any free year there is a one in twenty chance one begins; it lasts one to twelve years by its kind, and the world then rests six years. Nothing begins in a house's first fifteen years. Beginnings and ends are told in the journal, and the life screen names the event beside the weather while it runs. The events roll their own dice, keyed on the seed and the year. **A balance change:** the autopilot lives through them too. Battle deaths went from 4.1% to 4.0% over 960 lives (the band is 3 to 7%). Not built: events that change which creatures are met or how strong they are, events in one place only, and events the player or the factions cause.
20. **Hard-mode dynasty options.** Built (`GameHardMode`, `data/game/hard_mode.json`, the Hard mode button on the title screen). Three options can be switched on when a dynasty is founded, and only then: they cannot be added to a house that already lives and they can never be switched off. With whole-line permadeath, an heir slain in battle ends the dynasty; an heir who dies of old age or sickness still passes the name on. With a generation cap of 10, 25, 50 or 100, the saga ends when the heir of that generation dies. With an iron-man save, the house cannot be copied or exported as a backup, a backup of such a house is not read back in, nothing can be restored over its slot, and the save before the last is not kept. A house founded with none of them plays and saves exactly as before. Not built: replays do not record the options; a damaged iron-man save cannot be recovered; and the game cannot stop a player who copies the save file by hand or edits it.
21. **A family house that grows across generations.** Upgrades bought between hunts and passed down the line: more party slots, crafting, better training and shops. Idea from the base between expeditions in *The Shattered Isles*, fitted to the dynasty. Builds on item 18 (heirloom upgrades) and the persistent estates the README promises. Progress: the core rules and the first two tracks (Hall and Training Yard) are in, and the House button on the life screen buys them. The autopilot does not buy upgrades, so the battle figures do not move with the screen.
22. **Reactions that ask first.** When a hurt party member is about to be hit, the player is offered a reaction such as "Cover this ally?"; a member on Auto decides for itself. Idea from *The Shattered Isles*, fitted to party commands. It changes battle results, so it is a balance change: show before and after numbers. Progress: a standing version is in. In a party round each member's Cover button picks someone to cover; when a foe swings at that member below 30% of their health (`cover_below` in rounds.json), the coverer takes the blow instead, once a round. It is set before the round and kept from round to round, with no pause yet. The autopilot and auto-battle never set a cover, so their results and the balance figures do not change. The prompt is in too: when a foe is about to strike a member below 30%, the round stops and asks which member on manual control steps in front, once per covered member a round; "Let it land" plays exactly as before. Still to do: members on Auto deciding to cover for themselves, which changes results and needs balance numbers.

**Before the 2D/3D sandbox: groundwork**

Proposed order: items 23 to 26 first (they change how saves and views work, and get harder later), then the rendering trial (36), then the world systems in text (41 onwards) while the art catches up.

23. **Save versioning.** Done (`GameSaveVersion`). A save version number and a chain of tested upgrade steps from each version to the next. Today old saves load because new keys are optional; world chunks, inventories and positions need more than that.
24. **Saving in the middle of a fight.** Done (`GameDynasty.to_save`, `GameBattle` and `GameRound` `to_dict`/`restore`). A battle, and a round waiting on a question, saved and loaded exactly. A phone can close the app at any moment, and the sandbox needs mid-action state anyway.
25. **A fixed list of events for views.** Done for the battle: every typed event a fight emits is listed with its fields and kinds in `data/game/battle_events.json` (`GameEventSpec`), and `tests/test_game_event_list.gd` checks that each event played and each type in the source is on the list. Life actions return text lines and emit no typed events; they move to keyed data with item 28. The isometric view becomes a second renderer on the same events as the text screens.
26. **Cosmetic randomness kept apart.** Done: the view's dice are `CosmeticRng`'s (`ui/play/cosmetic_rng.gd`), and `tests/test_game_cosmetic.gd` checks that nothing in `ui/play` draws the dynasty's or Godot's global dice and that fights shown on the battle screen end exactly as fights with no view. Today only the scenery scatter rolls dice (keyed by place); particles and camera shake will use the same class.
27. **A world clock apart from actions.** Done (`GameClock`, `GameDynasty.pass_time`): one call passes time, whether an action passes whole years or a real-time world feeds seconds, which add up to year-long spans with the rest carried and saved. Ageing, wages, disease, weather, the death roll and the Ages run the same either way, and the clock holds during fights, waiting events and successions. A span of 1 year and 60 seconds a year are placeholders (`balance.json` "clock_*") to tune with the sandbox; shorter spans would need the per-call rolls (weather, the death roll, mana regeneration) scaled to the span first.
28. **Text out of code, and one data check.** Progress: the one data check is done (`tools/check_data.gd`, run by `tests/test_game_data.gd` and so by CI): every JSON file, every system's validator and the text catalog. Text is started with the fight: its 28 log lines are keys in `data/text/battle.json` read through `GameMessages`, and the logs of 960 fights came out byte for byte the same. Still to move: the life actions' messages (`game_dynasty.gd`, `game_events.gd`, `game_party.gd`, `game_house.gd` and the rest of `core/game`, about 140 more sites). The battle events list (item 25) is part of the data check.
29. **Replay files.** Done (`GameReplay`, `tools/replay.gd`): a run's founding and commands, with fingerprints, as one pasteable line, played back to exactly the same dynasty or to the command where it first differs. The autopilot and the tests record; the screens do not yet, so a player's own session cannot be captured until each screen's actions go through the recorder (the next step for player bug reports). The balance harness can use it to keep a seed's run as a regression.
30. **Long runs on a schedule, with budgets.** Done (`tools/long_run_check.gd`, `data/game/budgets.json`, `.github/workflows/long-runs.yml`): every night the autopilot plays 60 generations for each starting race and seed, one race per job, and each run is held to limits on save size (about 42 KB after 100 generations), load time (under 10 ms), time per generation (0.7 to 1.6 s) and the journal, and to the line carrying on; the battle-death band is judged when 400 or more lives were played. It can be started by hand with other seeds and generations. Replaying a failing seed uses item 29.
31. **Save files beyond the clipboard.** Progress: export and import of a save file is done. Export to file and Import from file on the title screen (and Export to file in the game menu) write and read a `.glsave` text file holding the same block as Copy backup (`GameSaves.export_file`/`import_file`), through the platform's file dialog where it has one and otherwise through the Downloads or `user://exports` folder. Still to do: the phone share sheet (it needs a native plugin) and optional cloud sync, and the file dialog and folder fallback are untested on a real Android or iOS device.

**Before the 2D/3D sandbox: art and presentation**

32. **Battle sprites from data.** Done. Pixel figures for every creature (the five bosses included), race and class, stored as text grids in `data/art/` and drawn on the battle screen in place of the blocks (`ui/play/sprite_art.gd`, `docs/ART.md`). The same art and pipeline feed the isometric world. Also done with it: every hero and companion wears a shirt colour of their own, picked for them or chosen on the Looks screen, and every fight is drawn in front of the land it is fought in, with its weather and season (`ui/play/scenery_art.gd`).
33. **Animation states.** Done as poses on the sprites, driven by the battle's events (`ui/play/figure_pose.gd`): figures breathe when standing, hop to attack and cast, flinch when hurt, step back to dodge, crouch to guard and fall when knocked out or slain, in whole pixels of the art. Reduced motion stills them. Drawn frames for a figure can replace a pose later.
34. **An art style guide and texture atlases.** Done. `docs/ART_STYLE.md` writes down the palette, outline, light, sizes and facing, with the numbers in `data/art/style.json`; the ones a machine can check (outline share, stray pixels, colour cap, size, margins, standing on the ground) are held for every class figure and creature by `SpriteArt.style_problems` (two creatures that broke the margin rule, the rift shade and the drake, were shifted a pixel to fit). Sprite textures are regions of shared 1024 x 1024 atlas pages (`ui/play/texture_atlas.gd`), uploaded once per screen with `SpriteArt.begin_batch()`/`end_batch()`. The scenery's large pictures are not in the atlas, and a page is never freed (the cache has always only grown).
35. **Screenshot tests.** A CI job that renders key screens under a virtual display and compares them with stored images, so changes to the UI or the art are seen in review.
36. **A rendering trial on a real phone.** A throwaway isometric pixel-art scene in Godot's 3D (orthographic camera, pixel snapping, sprites and lights) on the target Android phone: frame time, battery, heat and APK size. Then decide the technique, and how art files are stored (Git LFS, import settings), before making art for it.
37. **Graphics settings.** Built (`ui/play/graphics_settings.gd`, `data/ui/graphics.json`, the menu's Graphics rows). Three quality presets, Low, Medium and High, each a pixel scale (4, 3, 2), an effects level (off, some, all) and a frame cap (30, 60, uncapped); a computer starts on High and a phone on Medium. A **battery saver** lies over whichever is chosen: at most 30 frames a second, no effects, and the engine's low-processor mode, which redraws only when something changes. The choice is kept in the settings file, not the save, and applied at start and the moment it changes. Today the frame cap and the low-processor mode reach the engine, and the effects level decides whether a fight's painted scene shows rain, snow and embers (the rest of the scene is unchanged with them off). The pixel scale is stored and waits for the 3D renderer, which is what it is for. Still to do: more effects to switch (there is only the one), and measuring what the battery saver saves on a phone.
38. **Gear shown on figures.** Done. The heir's weapon and armour change their figure (`data/art/gear.json`): metal, wood and leather finishes land on the weapon or the armour only, magic weapons glow, and plate, vestments, shadow weave, dragonscale and barrow plate hang a cape. Gear drawn as its own layers (a different weapon shape, a helmet) is still to come.
39. **Input for a world.** First part built (`ui/play/world_input.gd`, `data/ui/input.json`; nothing reads it until there is a sandbox screen). The open world's 16 actions (move, look, zoom, dig, build, use, back, menu, drag the view) and their bindings are data: moving is on WASD, the arrows, the d-pad and the left stick; looking on IJKL and the right stick; zoom on the wheel, + and -, and the shoulders; dig on E, the left mouse button and X; build on R, the right mouse button and Y. The game installs them in the engine's input map at start, and the data check refuses an action a gamepad or the keyboard cannot reach and a key bound to two things. The arithmetic is done and tested: a stick's dead middle, a diagonal no faster than straight, a finger's stick drawn where it lands, a tap told from a drag or a hold, zoom by steps and by pinch within limits, the eight ways to face and the tile in front (what a gamepad or the keyboard digs and builds on). Reduced motion carries over: the camera does not glide. Still to do: the sandbox screen that reads it, tracking fingers on that screen, pointing at a tile with a mouse or finger (it needs the renderer's projection, item 36), on-screen buttons for dig and build on a phone, and remapping.
40. **Localisation.** First part built (`GameLocale`, `data/text/locales.json`). The text that is in data, the fight's 29 log lines and the tutorial's 14 tips, is now read through a locale: English, the source, reads exactly as before; a translation is a folder of line files that may reorder a line's placeholders but must use the same ones (a line that does not, or is missing, falls back to English, and the data check fails a listed language that is incomplete); and a **pseudo-language** made on the fly accents every letter, pads each line by a third and brackets it. The pseudo-language test runs the phone layouts at every text size with it and holds the tutorial's tip strip to 22% of the screen's height (it takes 17% at worst; a tip five times as long fails). The game's font is checked for every letter of western and central European languages. The language is kept in the settings file, and the menu grows a Language row once there is a second one. **Only English ships, and most of the game's text is still written in code** (the life actions, events, screens: item 28), so a translation today would change the fight's log and the tips and nothing else. Still to do: moving the rest of the text into catalogs, a first real translation, plural forms and names with grammar, number and date formats, a font for Chinese, Japanese and Korean, and right-to-left scripts.

**Before the 2D/3D sandbox: world systems, settled in text first**

The text game is the cheap place to get these rules right: they are core rules and data with headless tests, and a text screen where one helps.

41. **World spaces and coordinates.** First and second parts built (`GameSpaces`, `data/world/spaces.json`). Every place now has a space id and a position on the generated world. Ids are paths: `world` is the open world; each of the 17 authored places is `world/<place>` at its anchor tile, a town or a stretch of wilds as an area that is walked into, a dungeon as an interior behind a door; each town's services are interiors of the town (`world/hearthmere/forge`), 21 in all, and the family house is `world/hearthmere/house`, each with its own door on the town's cleared ground. From that the game can say where a space is on the map, which area a tile is in, where the heir is (from the text game's location), and the way from one space to another as leaving, walking and entering (house to the Goblin Warrens: out of the house, out of the town, across the world, into the dungeon); every one of the text game's roads is a walk between two tiles with the years it takes. It agrees with the generated world (each town's tile is dry land of the same name), the mine (the same root id) and the building claim (the house's door is on the family's ground). Nothing is saved. Second part built: each interior has rooms (a floor plan of one to four rooms with doors, checked by the validator, with `route_rooms` and the route walks across a floor); the underworld is a space of its bands (`world/underworld/underground` and `world/underworld/deep`), entered down a shaft and walked between by depth; each realm is a space with its gate on the open world, and a route through a gate is a `gate` step, open only when its flag is set (nothing here opens one); `route` and `along` give leave, walk, enter and gate steps and the position a fraction of the way along a route; `heir_at` gives the heir as a position. Still to do: the heir's travel state (the text game moves the heir at once, so no journey is held and nothing walks a route in the sandbox yet), a screen for any of it, and the underworld's own ground (the mine's gateway cells are not yet the shaft's tile, and nothing lives down there).
42. **One item and material list.** Done (`GameMaterials`, `data/items/materials.json`): 21 materials (tier 1 to 4, with tags such as metal, wood, cloth, precious, light, heavy, magic), and every item in `items.json` now names a `material` and a `weight`; its tags are the material's plus its slot or kind (`GameItems.tags_of`, `has_tag`, `material_of`, `weight_of`). The validator (in `tools/check_data.gd`) wants every item to reach a listed material, a weight, a tier in range, gear that does not stack and consumables that do, and every material and tag used. Nothing the game plays changed. Stable IDs are the existing lower-case ids, checked. Harvesting, mining, crafting (items 43 to 45) and the loot generator build on this list.
43. **Inventory with stacks and weight.** First part built (`GameCarry`, `data/items/carry.json`, on the weights of item 42): the heir carries what is worn, what is in the pack and a little for each healing potion, and can carry up to 40 plus 0.6 times their Strength. Past that nothing more is picked up by choice: a purchase is refused (the reason shown), and a find (the search of the dead, a made drop) is sold where it lies; gifts from events and quests are always taken, and may leave the heir over the limit with no other penalty. Stacks were already there (remedies, toxins and vials stack to their `stack` size) and each unit weighs its own. The pack screen has a Drop button (gear asks twice) and says how much is carried; the life screen's summary too. The autopilot is never over the limit (a test plays it), so its results and the golden digests do not move. Still to do: a penalty or a speed cost for a heavy load, carrying for the party and the house's stores, containers and bags that raise the limit, scrapping into materials (item 44), and weight of the creatures and goods the sandbox will move.
44. **Gathering and crafting.** First part built (`GameCraft`, `data/items/craft.json`, `ui/play/craft_panel.gd`). **There is no recipe grid: a piece is composed.** The heir picks a form, a material they have gathered and up to three essences, and the result is the same kind of made item the loot generator builds (an id `GameLoot.build` reads), so what is made and what drops are one system. Essences are affixes kept in a bottle: 0, 1, 2 or 3 of them make a common, fine, rare or legendary piece (a mythic cannot be crafted). **Gathering:** hunts leave materials (a table per creature, rolled on dice keyed on the seed, asked for by the screens only), a slain legend always leaves its parts, and now and then a creature leaves an essence (the wolf leaves the Wolf). **Salvaging:** break up gear in the pack for half its material and, from a made piece, its affixes as essences (a pelt keeps the wolf), so a full pack becomes parts. The stores belong to the family and pass down (`GameDynasty.stores`, saved only once something is in them). Crafting needs a forge (a town); salvaging works anywhere. Cost: material by the piece's weight, plus a fee of 15% of its price in gold. Quality 0 to 9 comes from the heir's level, Craft quality traits (Craftmaster's Hands, Artificer) and a keyed roll, and is shown before the piece is made; a made piece takes the world's grade. 45. **Mining as data.** First part built, headless (`GameMine`, `data/mining/mining.json`; no screen yet). **The ground:** rock layers by depth (topsoil, clay, stone, deep stone, darkrock, ember rock, worldroot) with a hardness and whether ore forms there, grouped into the GDD's three zones (the Surface, Underground, the Deep), down to depth 400; and digging down goes back in time (`age_at`: the dynasty's own Age at the surface, one older for each 60 deep, none before the first Age). **Ore** grows in veins: each 8 by 8 by 8 block may hold one vein, a sphere whose kind comes from depth bands (copper and iron near the surface, then crystal and silver, gold, mithril, and starmetal only in the Deep), so the deeper it is the rarer and better: about 7.6% of cells near the surface are ore, 0.65% at the bottom. 46. **World generation from the dynasty's seed.** First part built, headless (`GameWorldGen`, `data/world/worldgen.json`; no screen yet). **The authored map is the skeleton:** the 17 places of `world.json` are fixed **anchors** laid on a 640 by 640 tile grid (the map's x and y times 64), so the sandbox agrees with the text game's map and the generator never moves or contradicts a place; an anchor's own biome is guaranteed even with the border warped hard (tested). **Between anchors** the land is the same for the same dynasty seed: eight surface biomes (plains, forest, hills, mountains, swamp, tundra, coast, volcanic; the map's cave, crypt, ruins, road, fae and rift places stand on the surface biome their `surface_of` names), found from the nearest anchors with a noise warp on the borders; heights blend the nearest biomes' and add fractal noise, so there are no cliffs (neighbouring tiles differ by 14 at most, nearly always 4 or less); the sea takes what falls below sea level and the edge of the world. **Towns and dungeons are flat, dry and clear of nodes** around the anchor (the GDD's anchor zones). **Nodes** (oak and yew trees, herb patches, bone piles, rock outcrops) are placed tile by tile from the biome's table and a clumping noise into groves, with the wood the game's oak and yew now have a source in the world; an **outcrop** shows only where the mining ground (item 45) has ore within a few cells of the surface and is of that ore, so the surface hints at what lies below. Needs creature data with a `farm` flag (kept out of fight and Explore pools), a price list of goods, and art for each animal. Item 56's wool, eggs and venom work is a stand-in: wool is on the frost wolf only because the game has no sheep yet, and moves to the sheep when they exist. **Built:** seven farm animals (sheep, cattle, pigs, chickens, goats, geese, horses, with art; a horse hauls and is never slaughtered), kept out of every fight and find; bought young at a town's market; raised and bred like any creature; wool, milk and eggs by the season and by how well they are kept; slaughter for meat and hide (asks twice). Wool moved from the frost wolf to the sheep. Still to do: bees (a hive needs its own rules), prices that vary with the market, trade, and the goods having a market of their own.
63. **A loot generator with no item list.** First part built (`GameLoot`, `data/items/loot.json`, on the material list of item 42). A made item is built from its own id, `gen:<rarity>:<slot>:<form>:<material>:<affixes>:<mythic>:<grade>:<quality>`, out of 15 forms, the materials, 24 affixes (prefix and suffix) and 6 rarities, so a save holds only the id and `GameItems.item_def` hands the built item to everything that knows items (wearing, selling, tags, weapon kind, saving). **It grows with the world:** material tiers open at generations 1, 6, 14 and 30, and after that every 12 generations add a grade (+1, +2, up to +20) that strengthens all effects, so there is always something better. **Rarity** is a roll out of a million: common 70%, fine 23%, rare 6%, epic 0.9%, legendary 0.099%, and **mythic ten in a million**, with a name of its own (Dawnbringer, Aegis of Ages, the First Crown) and the strongest effects in the game; a slain legend leaves one drop at 25 times the odds of the rare ones. Drops are asked for by the screens only (the search of the dead, a slain legend), on dice keyed on the seed, so the autopilot and the golden digests do not move. ## After the text game: the 2D/3D sandbox

Starts only once the text game is complete. The text game's systems (lineage, Fate, battles, diseases, companions, Ages) carry over: logic stays apart from rendering, so the new world is a new view on the same core.

### Look and shape
- An **open-world sandbox**, in the spirit of Minecraft, Terraria and RimWorld.
- **Isometric pixel-art 2D, running on 3D code**: a real 3D world drawn as isometric pixel art, so height, digging down and building up are real.

### Harvesting
- Wood, rock, plants, food and fruit, meat from creatures, ores, minerals, gems and many kinds of fluids.
- **Finite nodes that come back.** A tree or an ore vein gives a few items, then disappears. It respawns after a while, unless the player has built over the spot.

### Mining
- Mining is a headline system that should go beyond any other mining game: depth, strata, what lies under the ground and how digging changes the world.
- **Digging into the underworld.** Mining down from the overworld slowly changes the ground around the shaft until it becomes a gateway: the player passes from the overworld into the underworld and the two connect there.

### Crafting
- A **one-of-a-kind crafting system**, designed to stand on its own rather than copy a recipe grid.

### Worlds and spaces
- **Gateways to other worlds** (the realms).
- **World-space IDs.** Every space has its own ID. Entering a building loads its interior as its own space, like a small dimension of its own. The same mechanism carries the underworld and the other worlds.
- **Creatures across the worlds.** Raised and bred creatures travel by the same gateways, and can be crossed with creatures of other realms (roadmap item 58): the ranch is a place in a world, and a pen's creatures live by that world's flow of time.

### Mods
- **Mod support.** Players can load their own mods or other people's, each in their own game. Content is already data-driven (JSON), which is the base for this.
